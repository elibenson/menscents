Fragrance families explained for beginners

Fresh, woody, aromatic, amber and gourmand: a usable map of scent language.

Fresh

Fresh fragrances may evoke citrus, watery effects, green leaves, or clean laundry. These descriptions communicate an impression rather than an ingredient list. They can be useful starting points for daytime and warmer-weather shopping.

Woody

Woody fragrances can suggest cedar, sandalwood, vetiver, dry timber, or smoky materials. Some feel airy and understated; others feel dense and resinous. The word woody alone does not tell you how heavy a fragrance will be.

Aromatic

Aromatic compositions often recall herbs such as lavender, rosemary, sage, or mint. Many familiar men’s fragrances blend aromatic notes with citrus and woods. It is a broad family rather than a strict category.

Amber and gourmand

Amber is often used for warm, sweet, resinous, or vanilla-like effects; gourmand suggests edible associations such as caramel, coffee, or dessert. Neither label guarantees a particular ingredient or performance.

Use families as a filter, not a verdict

If you like one woody scent, you may dislike another. Use family descriptions to narrow your sampling list, then let the actual formula make the decision.
