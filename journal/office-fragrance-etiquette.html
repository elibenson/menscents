The unwritten rules of office fragrance

How to smell good at work without making fragrance everyone else’s problem.

Shared air changes the equation

A workplace includes people with different preferences and sensitivities. Fragrance should not dominate elevators, meeting rooms, or desks.

Aim for close-range enjoyment

Choose a fragrance you enjoy at a modest application level. A restrained woody, citrus, or aromatic profile can be a useful starting point, but no scent family guarantees subtlety.

Respect fragrance-free policies

Some workplaces and healthcare settings have fragrance restrictions. Follow the actual policy rather than assuming a “clean” fragrance is exempt.

Ask for useful feedback

If you are unsure whether a fragrance is noticeable, ask a trusted colleague discreetly. Do not treat compliments as the only measure of success.

Keep the objective simple

The goal is to feel put together, not to announce your arrival from across the room.

Make a considered choice

Explore the fragrance index, try the Scent Finder, or compare two fragrances. These guides are research-led and do not represent hands-on testing.
