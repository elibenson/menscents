Eau de Toilette vs. Eau de Parfum.

Fragrance concentration names are useful labels, but they do not guarantee strength, quality or longevity.

Concentration is not a quality ranking

Eau de Toilette and Eau de Parfum commonly refer to different fragrance concentrations, but there is no universal industry-wide percentage that guarantees how every bottle will smell or perform. Brands may reformulate the scent itself between versions.

Expect a different composition, not just more oil

An EDP is not always an EDT with extra fragrance concentrate. The two may emphasize different notes, textures, or accords. Compare the specific bottles rather than assuming the labels describe one formula at two strengths.

Projection and longevity are different

Projection is how readily other people notice a fragrance at a distance; longevity is how long you perceive it. Neither can be predicted reliably from the EDT or EDP label alone.

Test the drydown

Try each version on separate days in similar conditions. Check the opening, the scent after an hour, and whether you still enjoy wearing it later. Your preferences matter more than a generic concentration hierarchy.

What to buy

Choose the version whose smell, price, and intended use fit you. If you cannot test both, start with a sample rather than assuming the more concentrated-sounding name is the better purchase.
