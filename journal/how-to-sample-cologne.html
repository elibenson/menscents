How to sample cologne before buying

A practical method for testing fragrance on skin without letting the first spray make the decision.

Start on paper, finish on skin

A paper strip is useful for eliminating fragrances you dislike immediately. It is not a substitute for wearing the scent. Try no more than two unfamiliar fragrances on skin in one session, ideally one on each forearm, so that you can remember which is which.

Give the opening time to settle

The first minutes are often dominated by volatile materials. Revisit the scent after roughly half an hour and again later in the day. Notice whether you still enjoy the drydown rather than judging only the opening.

Test in your actual routine

A fragrance that feels perfect in a cool store may behave differently during a warm commute or long workday. Wear a sample on an ordinary day, then on the occasion you actually plan to use it.

Ask three questions before paying

Do I enjoy the fragrance after the opening? Does it suit the places I intend to wear it? Would I choose it over what I already own? If the answer to any is uncertain, a sample or travel size is a better next purchase than a full bottle.

Avoid false precision

A stranger’s longevity report is not a guarantee for your skin. Formulation, environment, dose, and perception vary. Record your own experience rather than treating online hour counts as specifications.
