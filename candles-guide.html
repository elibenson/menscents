How to choose a scented candle

A good candle should suit the room, your routine and your tolerance for scent.

Scent comes before packaging

An elegant vessel looks good on a shelf, but the fragrance itself matters more. Read the scent description and note family, then consider a smaller size before investing in a large candle.

Understand the tradeoffs

Wax type alone does not guarantee a cleaner or longer burn. Vessel shape, wick selection, fragrance load, burn conditions and care all affect how a candle behaves. Treat burn-time claims as manufacturer estimates rather than universal guarantees.

Consider the space

A living room may suit a richer woody or amber scent, while a compact home office may benefit from a softer profile. Ventilation and personal sensitivity affect how strong a candle feels.

Care and fire safety

Follow the manufacturer’s instructions. Trim the wick to roughly one-quarter inch when appropriate, keep the flame away from combustibles, use a stable heat-resistant surface, and never leave a burning candle unattended. Extinguish it before leaving the room or going to sleep.

When not to choose a candle

If you cannot supervise a flame, have curious pets or children, or want constant background scent, consider another approach. A candle is not the right default for every household.
